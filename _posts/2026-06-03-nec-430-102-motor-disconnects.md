# NEC 430.102 Motor Disconnect Requirements

## Overview

NEC 430.102 covers the required location of disconnecting means for motors and motor controllers.

In the field, this matters because a wall key switch, push button, selector switch, remote, or pendant station should not automatically be treated as the required energy-isolating disconnecting means for servicing motor equipment.

A control device may start and stop the motor, but that does not automatically make it the NEC-required motor disconnect.

## Practical Field Issue

For ceiling-mounted motors, such as basketball goal motors in a gymnasium, the question is usually whether the disconnecting means is:

- properly rated,
- accessible,
- within sight where required,
- lockable open where permitted,
- suitable as a motor disconnect,
- and capable of opening the ungrounded conductors to the motor or controller.

## Control Device vs Disconnecting Means

A key switch, selector switch, push button, pendant control, or remote-control station is normally a control device.

A control device is not the same thing as a disconnecting means unless it is specifically listed, rated, and installed for that purpose.

The disconnecting means must be suitable for disconnecting the motor circuit. It should not be assumed that a normal operating control qualifies as the required NEC motor disconnect.

## Within Sight Requirement

The motor disconnecting means is generally required to be within sight from the motor location and the driven machinery location.

For basketball goal motors, this can become a problem when the motor is located above the gym floor and the disconnect is located in another room, electrical room, hallway, or panel location.

If the disconnect is not within sight, the installation needs to be reviewed carefully under the applicable NEC rule, exception, lockable-open provisions, and AHJ interpretation.

## Lockable Disconnects

A disconnect located away from the motor may be allowed in certain cases when it is capable of being locked in the open position.

The lockable provision should comply with NEC 110.25.

The lock must remain in place with or without the lock installed. A removable padlock alone is not enough if the equipment does not have a proper lockable-open provision.

## NEC 430.102 Exception No. 2

Exception No. 2 to NEC 430.102 is narrow and should not be treated as a general permission to put motor disconnects anywhere.

This exception is generally aimed at industrial-type installations where qualified persons service the equipment and written safety procedures are in place.

For a typical gymnasium basketball goal motor installation, this exception may not be a good fit unless all of the required conditions are clearly met and accepted by the AHJ.

## Common Mistakes

Common mistakes include:

- treating a wall key switch as the motor disconnect,

- treating a push button or pendant as the disconnect,
- installing the disconnect only in the electrical room without checking the within-sight rule,
- assuming a breaker in a panel is acceptable without checking lockable-open requirements,
- failing to verify whether the device is listed and rated as a motor disconnect,
- and ignoring the difference between control and energy isolation.

## Field Recommendation

For service and maintenance safety, the best practice is to provide a properly rated disconnecting means within sight of the motor or driven machinery whenever practical.

Where that is not practical, the installer, inspector, and AHJ should verify that the remote disconnect arrangement fully complies with NEC 430.102, NEC 110.25, equipment listing instructions, and any adopted local amendments.
